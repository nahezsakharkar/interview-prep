# Salary Negotiation

## Definition

Salary negotiation is the process of aligning your compensation with your market value and the value you bring to the company. It is a business conversation, not a personal request.

## The Core Principles

### 1. Never give a number first
The first person to name a number sets the "anchor." If you name a number too low, you leave money on the table. If you name it too high, you might price yourself out.
- **The Pivot**: "I'm more interested in finding a role that is a great fit. I'm sure we can agree on a fair market rate once we both decide I'm the right person for the job."

### 2. Base the request on Market Value, not Personal Need
Never say "I need X because my rent increased." Instead, say "Based on my research for Senior Full-Stack roles in [City/Industry] with my specific experience in [GenAI/Fintech], the market range is X to Y."

### 3. Negotiate a Package, not just a Base Salary
If the base salary is capped, look at other levers:
- **Signing Bonus**: A one-time payment to bridge the gap.
- **Equity (RSUs/Options)**: Long-term wealth creation.
- **Performance Bonus**: Tied to measurable KPIs.
- **Vacation/Flexibility**: More PTO or a fully remote arrangement.

## The Negotiation Process

### Stage 1: The Initial Screen
The recruiter will ask for your "expectations."
- **Response**: "I'm currently focusing on the role and the impact I can have. I'd prefer to discuss compensation after I've had a chance to learn more about the requirements and the team. Do you have a range for this position?"

### Stage 2: The Offer
You receive the offer. **Do not accept immediately.**
- **Response**: "Thank you so much for the offer! I'm very excited about the possibility of joining the team. I'd like to take a day or two to review the full package and discuss it with my family. Can I get back to you by [Date]?"

### Stage 3: The Counter-Offer
If the offer is below your target, present a data-backed counter.
- **The Script**: "I'm very excited about the role and the team. However, based on the current market for my skill set—specifically my experience with [Specific High-Value Skill]—I was expecting something closer to [Target Number]. If we can get closer to that, I'm ready to sign today."

## Working Example: The "Value-Add" Pivot

When a recruiter says "That's above our budget," don't just drop your price. Pivot to the value you provide.

**Incorrect**: "Okay, I can do [Lower Number] instead."
**Correct**: "I understand the budget constraints. However, given my experience in [Area X], I can hit the ground running and eliminate the need for [Expensive External Tool/Consultant] or reduce the onboarding time for the rest of the team. I believe that value justifies the [Target Number]. Is there any flexibility in the signing bonus or equity to make this work?"

## Common Pitfalls

- **Being overly aggressive**: Negotiation should be a collaborative process. Maintain a tone of "I want to work here, let's find a way to make it happen."
- **Lying about other offers**: It's a small world. If you claim to have another offer to create leverage, be prepared for the recruiter to ask for details or for the offer to be withdrawn if they suspect dishonesty.
- **Forgetting the "Whole Package"**: Focusing only on the base salary and ignoring a generous equity grant that could be worth significantly more in the long run.

## Interview questions (Internal)

- **What is my "walk-away" number?** (The absolute minimum you will accept).
- **What is my "happy" number?** (The number that makes you say 'Yes' immediately).
- **What are my non-monetary priorities?** (Remote work, title, specific project ownership).
