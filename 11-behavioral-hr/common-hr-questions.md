# Common HR questions

## 1. Tell me about yourself

**Framework**

- Current role and experience
- What you have built
- Why this role fits your goals

**Good answer pattern**

> I am a software engineer with X years of experience in frontend/backend systems. I have worked on Y, where I handled Z. I am now looking for opportunities that combine product ownership, architecture, and growth in a fast-moving engineering team.

## 2. Why do you want to leave your current role?

Keep it positive and growth-oriented.

**Good answer pattern**

- You want more challenge and scale
- The new role is a better match for your career goals
- You are grateful for current experience, but the next step is strategically better

## 3. Why do you want this role?

Tie the opportunity to the actual business and team context.

- Match skills to job requirements
- Show interest in the product or platform
- Explain why the team and stage matter to you

## 4. What are your strengths?

Choose 2-3 strengths and show evidence.

**Framework**

- Strength
- Example of behavior
- Result

## 5. What is your biggest weakness?

Pick a real weakness, but one you are actively improving.

**Good answer pattern**

> I used to be too detail-oriented in early feature work, which slowed execution. I have improved by setting clearer review checkpoints and prioritizing the highest-value decisions sooner.

## 6. Describe a conflict you had at work

Use STAR and focus on resolution.

## 7. Tell me about a time you failed

Keep it honest and reflective.

- What happened
- What you learned
- What you improved

## 8. Tell me about a time you led without authority

Show influence and communication.

## 9. Tell me about a time you delivered under pressure

Highlight prioritization, calm trade-offs, and clear communication.

## 10. Why should we hire you?

Focus on value, not self-praise.

**Good answer pattern**

- direct contribution to the role
- examples of impact
- how your strengths match the team gap

## 11. Where do you see yourself in 5 years?

Be realistic and role-aligned.

## 12. What motivates you?

Do not give a vague answer; connect it to ownership and growth.

## 13. How do you handle feedback?

Show humility plus action.

## 14. What do you do when a project is at risk?

Focus on escalation, clarity, and recovery steps.

## 15. Do you have any questions for us?

Ask thoughtful, role-specific questions.

**Examples**

- How is the team structured?
- What are the biggest challenges this team is solving?
- What does success look like in the first 6 months?

## Interview advice

- Use STAR structure for every example.
- Keep stories concise but concrete.
- Quantify outcomes when possible.
- Tie the answer back to the role you are interviewing for.
