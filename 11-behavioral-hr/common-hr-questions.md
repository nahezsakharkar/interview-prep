# Common HR questions

This guide provides structured frameworks for answering behavioral and HR questions. The goal is to move from "generic" answers to "evidence-backed" narratives.

## 1. Tell me about yourself
**Framework: Past $\rightarrow$ Present $\rightarrow$ Future**
- **Past**: A brief mention of your foundation (Education/Early roles).
- **Present**: Your current role, the scale of the systems you manage, and your primary technical focus.
- **Future**: Why you are looking for a change and how this specific role is the logical next step.

**Example Answer**:
> "I'm a Software Engineer with about 2.5 years of experience, primarily in financial services. Currently, I'm focused on building agentic AI interfaces and modernizing legacy systems—most recently leading a migration from Angular to React for a core financial dashboard. I've developed a strong interest in the intersection of GenAI and UX, which is why I'm excited about this role; it allows me to apply my full-stack experience to build AI-driven products at scale."

## 2. Why do you want to leave your current role?
**The Golden Rule**: Never speak negatively about your current or past employer. Frame the exit as "moving toward" something rather than "running away" from something.

**Positive Framing**:
- **Growth**: "I've learned a great deal about [Skill X], and now I'm looking to apply that to a larger scale/different domain."
- **Challenge**: "I'm looking for a role with more ownership over the architectural decisions of the product."
- **Alignment**: "My current role is focused on [A], but my long-term career goal is to specialize in [B], which is exactly what this team is doing."

## 3. Why do you want this role / this company?
**Framework: Company Value + Role Fit + Personal Interest**
- **The "Why You"**: Mention a specific feature, engineering blog post, or product challenge the company has.
- **The "Why Me"**: Connect a specific requirement in the job description to a project in your resume.
- **The "Match"**: Explain how this role fits into your 2-year growth plan.

**Example**:
> "I've been following [Company]'s work on [Feature X], and I was impressed by how you handle [Technical Challenge]. In my previous project, I solved a similar problem by [Brief Action], and I'm eager to bring that experience to a team that is operating at your scale."

## 4. What are your strengths?
**Framework: Strength $\rightarrow$ Evidence $\rightarrow$ Impact**
Avoid adjectives like "hardworking" or "passionate." Use "proven" strengths.

**Examples**:
- **Technical Versatility**: "I can move seamlessly between a Python backend and a React frontend. For example, when building GenUI Studio, I designed both the orchestration service and the dynamic renderer, which reduced the need for hand-offs between teams."
- **Ownership**: "I don't just deliver tickets; I look for systemic improvements. I noticed our regression tests were taking 3 days, so I built an automation framework that reduced it to 2 hours."

## 5. What is your biggest weakness?
**Framework: Real Weakness $\rightarrow$ Mitigation $\rightarrow$ Progress**
Avoid "fake" weaknesses like "I'm a perfectionist."

**Example**:
> "Early in my career, I tended to dive straight into coding before fully mapping out the edge cases, which occasionally led to rework. To fix this, I've adopted a 'design-first' approach where I write a brief technical spec and get it reviewed by a peer before starting the implementation. This has significantly reduced my bug rate and improved my delivery predictability."

## 6. Describe a conflict you had at work
**Framework: STAR (Focus on Resolution)**
The interviewer is testing your emotional intelligence (EQ) and professionalism.

- **S/T**: A disagreement over a technical choice (e.g., Redux vs Context API).
- **Action**: "I didn't push my opinion. Instead, I proposed we create a small prototype of both approaches and evaluate them against three criteria: bundle size, dev velocity, and readability."
- **Result**: "We chose [Option X] based on the data, and the team felt the decision was fair and objective."

## 7. Tell me about a time you failed
**Framework: Honest Mistake $\rightarrow$ Immediate Fix $\rightarrow$ Long-term Prevention**
Show that you are reflective and take ownership.

- **Mistake**: "I once deployed a change that caused a performance dip in a specific edge case because I hadn't tested it with a large enough dataset."
- **Fix**: "As soon as the alert fired, I rolled back the change and coordinated with the team to find the root cause."
- **Prevention**: "I then added a performance benchmark to our CI pipeline to ensure that no future change could regress that specific metric."

## 8. Tell me about a time you led without authority
**Framework: Identification $\rightarrow$ Influence $\rightarrow$ Outcome**
Show you can drive results through persuasion and expertise.

- **Situation**: "The team was using a fragmented set of tools for deployment."
- **Action**: "I didn't mandate a change, but I spent a weekend building a small proof-of-concept showing how [Tool X] could automate our workflow. I presented it during a lunch-and-learn."
- **Result**: "The team adopted the tool, and we saw a [X%] reduction in deployment errors."

## 9. What do you do when a project is at risk?
**Framework: Communication $\rightarrow$ Prioritization $\rightarrow$ Recovery**
- **Communication**: "First, I flag the risk to the stakeholders as early as possible to avoid surprises."
- **Prioritization**: "I work with the Product Manager to identify the 'Must-Haves' vs 'Nice-to-Haves' to ensure the core value is delivered on time."
- **Recovery**: "I analyze the bottleneck—whether it's a technical blocker or a resource issue—and propose a plan to resolve it (e.g., simplifying a feature or redistributing tasks)."

## 10. Why should we hire you?
**Framework: Value Proposition (The "Closing Pitch")**
Summarize your unique intersection of skills.

**Example**:
> "You should hire me because I bring a unique combination of senior-level full-stack engineering and hands-on experience with agentic AI. I've already built the exact type of orchestration and DSL systems this role requires, and I have a proven track record of reducing technical debt and improving developer velocity in financial services environments."

## 11. Do you have any questions for us?
**The "High-Signal" Questions**
Asking these shows you are thinking like a senior engineer.

- **On Team Culture**: "How does the team handle technical disagreements? Is there a formal RFC process?"
- **On Success**: "If I am successful in this role, what will I have achieved 6 months from now that would make you say 'this was a great hire'?"
- **On Technical Debt**: "Every fast-growing company has technical debt. What is the team's current strategy for balancing new feature delivery with stability and refactoring?"
- **On Growth**: "What is the most common path for engineers on this team to grow into more senior or architectural roles?"
